# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are talking to is acting in good faith, is working with
less context than you have, and is doing this in time they chose to give away.

## What is expected

- **Criticise the work, not the person.** "This duplicates the rule in `theme.rs`" is review.
  "You clearly did not read the code" is not, and it does not become review by being correct.
- **Accept that a change can be declined.** On scope, on the constitution, on simplicity. A
  maintainer saying no to a good idea is not saying anything about the person who had it.
- **Ask before assuming.** A great deal of friction in small projects is two people using one word
  for different things.
- **Respect the time others give.** Answer the questions asked, provide what a form asks for, and
  do not press for a faster response than a best-effort project can offer.

## What is not

Harassment in any form; personal, demeaning or discriminatory remarks — including about anyone's
background, identity, beliefs or experience; sexualised language or imagery; publishing anyone's
private information; sustained disruption of discussion; and encouraging any of the above.

This applies everywhere the project is: issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments,
discussions, and any space where you are participating as a contributor to hypr-swap.

## Reporting

Report a violation privately to **seraf_ac@hotmail.com**, the project maintainer's address. Include
what happened, where, and links if you have them. You do not need to be the person it happened to.

Reports are read by the maintainer alone and are not shared further without your agreement, except
where the law requires it. You can expect an acknowledgement within **five days**.

**A report about the maintainer's own conduct is a problem this channel cannot solve**, and saying
so is more honest than pretending otherwise: this is a single-maintainer project, so there is no
second person to escalate to. Raising it publicly in the issue tracker is legitimate, and so is
taking it to GitHub, whose [Community Guidelines](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-community-guidelines)
apply to this repository regardless of what this document says.

## What happens next

The maintainer will look into it and decide what to do — anything from a private word, to editing
or removing content, to blocking an account from the repository. The outcome is communicated to the
person who reported it. Severity, intent and whether it has happened before all bear on the
response; a single graceless comment and a pattern of them are not the same thing.
